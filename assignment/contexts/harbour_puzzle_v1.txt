                         THE HARBOURMASTER'S LEDGER OF THABILSOUND
                               Volume I -- The Twenty Berths
============================================================================================

This book is the standing record of the twenty berths of Thabilsound, kept by the
harbourmaster and copied fair at the close of each quarter. It is set down here in the form
the outer office requires, which is to say: a roster of what is on the roll, followed by the
entries taken on the rounds, followed by whatever notes the clerks thought worth keeping.

The berths are numbered from 1 to 20 and always in the same direction -- berth 1 is the
westernmost on the reach, berth 20 the easternmost. West therefore means a lower berth
number and east a higher one, and an entry that speaks of walking or sailing east is
speaking of counting upward. Nothing in this book uses any other convention.

Seven things are recorded against every berth, and there are exactly twenty of each on the
roll. Every berth has one keeper and no berth shares a keeper with another. The same holds
of the creature kept at the berth, of the cargo standing against it, of the home port
declared for it, of the pennant flown from it, of the ailment entered against it in the
quarter's health return, and of the watch shift it is worked under. The roster in Part One
lists all seven rolls in full; it does not say which berth any of them belongs to, and that
is the whole of what this book is for.

The numbered entries in Part Two are the round record. Every one of them is true as written.
They were taken by different clerks on different days and in no particular order, and many
of them say the same thing twice over in different words, which is the ordinary condition of
a ledger and not an error in it. Between the entries the clerks have set down the state of
the tide, the weather, the condition of the quay and whatever else was passing; none of that
bears on the berths and none of it should be read as though it did.

Taken together the numbered entries are sufficient to settle the whole table -- which
keeper, which creature, which cargo, which home port, which pennant, which ailment and which
watch stands at each of the twenty berths -- and to settle it one way only. That is the
exercise.
